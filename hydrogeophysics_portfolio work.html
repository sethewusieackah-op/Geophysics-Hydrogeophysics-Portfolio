Hydrogeophysical Investigation of Termitaria as Biomarkers of Groundwater

2D Electrical Resistivity Tomography (ERT) using the Schlumberger array to delineate subsurface hydrological conditions and evaluate termite mounds as surface indicators for groundwater exploration.

01. Study Overview

Research Summary

Groundwater exploration in complex hydrogeological environments requires efficient pre-drilling indicators. Termitaria exhibit distinct soil and hydraulic modifications. This study maps subsurface electrical resistivity distributions directly beneath mound locations to quantify their correlation with localized groundwater recharge and saturated aquifers.

Site A (Profiles 1 & 2)

160m profile lengths mapped low apparent resistivity zones (< 124 Ω·m) extending from shallow depths down to 31.5m directly beneath termitaria structures.

Site B (Profiles 3 & 4)

400m profile lengths identified deep saturated material between 20m and 52.3m depth with low apparent resistivity (< 93.3 Ω·m), aligning with typical groundwater resistivity ranges (10 – 100 Ω·m).

02. Method & Physics

Data Acquisition & Mathematical Physics

The Schlumberger array configuration uses two outer current electrodes (A, B) and two inner potential electrodes (M, N). Apparent resistivity (\rho_a) is derived from measured potential differences and geometric factors:

$$\rho_a = \pi \frac{L^2}{2l} \left( \frac{\Delta V}{I} \right)$$

L: Half-distance between current electrodes (A, B)

l: Half-distance between potential electrodes (M, N)

ΔV: Measured voltage difference across potential electrodes (mV)

I: Current injected into the subsurface (mA)

Field Procedure & Modeling

  1. Site Reconnaissance: Mapping termitaria positions, vegetation distribution, and establishing profile lines across target mounds.
  2. Data Acquisition: ABEM Terrameter SAS 4000 system with multi-electrode selector cables and stainless steel ground electrodes.
  3. Quality Control: Measurement stacking to eliminate background electrical noise and transient ground currents.
  4. Inversion Modeling: Finite-difference Gauss-Newton inversion using RES2DINV software (6 iterations, achieving low RMS errors of 2.3% – 8.7%).
